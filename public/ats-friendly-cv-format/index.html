How to format an ATS-friendly CV

Many employers route applications through an applicant tracking system (ATS) that reads text out of your file. A simple layout is easier to parse than a heavily designed one. This guide covers formatting habits that help, then how to export a PDF with Free CV Maker.

Steps

  1. Use a single-column layout. Start from a simple template. Avoid text in graphics, tables for your whole career history, and information that exists only in a header image or footer logo.
  2. Write standard section headings. Use familiar words such as Experience, Education, and Skills. Unusual headings can be missed by automated parsers and by tired human readers.
  3. Put key facts in ordinary text. Job titles, employer names, dates, and skills should be typed as text, not drawn as icons. Spell out months and years clearly.
  4. Export a regular PDF. In Free CV Maker, fill the form and export a PDF. Open the file and try selecting the text. If you can highlight a line, a parser can usually read it too.

What ATS-friendly does not mean

It does not mean stuffing the file with keywords or promising that a system will rank you first. A human still has to want to interview you. Match the language of the job post honestly and keep the document easy to scan.

File naming and length

Use a clear file name, such as your name plus CV. One or two pages is typical for many roles; follow the employer's instructions when they give a limit.
